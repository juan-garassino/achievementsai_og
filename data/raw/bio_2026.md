# Section 1: Early Life and Education

The subject of this biography was born in **Buenos Aires, Argentina**, a vibrant city known for its rich cultural heritage, European-influenced architecture, and bustling arts scene. Buenos Aires sits on the western shore of the **Rio de la Plata**, one of the widest estuaries in the world, historically significant as the gateway to the interior of South America during colonial times. Growing up in this dynamic city exposed him early to a mix of history, culture, and urban design that subtly shaped his sensibilities.

His family background is diverse and deeply rooted in history. On his mother's side, the family is primarily of **Italian origin**, which influenced his early exposure to European languages and culture. On his father's side, the genealogy is more complex: his paternal grandmother's lineage traces back to the first settlements in the Rio de la Plata region, predating Argentina's independence. This ancestry became a source of pride and inspired an appreciation for history, continuity, and the way past influences the present.

Growing up in Buenos Aires, in the residential neighborhood of **Nuñez**, he was deeply shaped by his parents’ professions. Nuñez, located along the northern edge of the city near the Rio de la Plata, offered wide streets, small parks, and easy access to the riverfront, providing a balance between urban life and open spaces. His mother, an architect specializing in **Construction Planning and Scheduling**, fostered his early curiosity about creative problem-solving and design thinking. She encouraged him to observe the cityscape and notice patterns, proportions, and relationships between buildings and public spaces. His father, a data engineer responsible for database infrastructure, introduced him to structured reasoning, logic, and the potential of technology to solve complex problems. This combination of creativity and analytical thinking laid a foundation for the interdisciplinary approach that would later characterize his career.

He attended both primary and secondary school in the same institution within Nuñez. The supportive environment encouraged independence and social skills, with daily routines that included playing outdoors, riding bikes, and exploring nearby streets and parks along the riverfront. Beyond academics, he engaged in various extracurricular activities that enriched his personal development. He studied **additional languages**, fostering an early interest in communication and cultural exchange, and learned to play **musical instruments**, which nurtured discipline, creativity, and patience. He also participated in **arts classes**, exploring drawing, painting, and design, further developing his sensitivity to aesthetics and form. These experiences collectively nurtured curiosity, resilience, spatial awareness, and a multidisciplinary mindset—skills that would later influence his architectural and AI work.

During high school, he excelled in the hard sciences, particularly physics and mathematics, while maintaining interest in chemistry, biology, history, and geography. Physics became his greatest passion, reflecting a natural inclination for understanding systems, patterns, and underlying principles. Literature was less captivating, though he maintained appreciation for social sciences. Career advisors recommended paths such as electronic engineering, biochemistry, or architecture, aligning with both his interests and parental influences.

Upon completing high school, after careful consideration and a conversation with his father, he chose to pursue **architecture at the University of Buenos Aires**, making the decision on the very day of university registration in 2005. The University of Buenos Aires, consistently ranked among the top 100 universities globally, offered rigorous technical education, lessons in perseverance, and exposure to a massive, diverse student body of around 50,000 students. Navigating this large academic environment taught him self-reliance, adaptability, and the ability to thrive in competitive settings.

During his fifth year, he studied **parametric design**, an algorithmic approach to architectural thinking, abroad at the **École des Beaux-Arts Malachais in Paris** for six months. To prepare, he dedicated a year to learning French, facilitated by his prior knowledge of Italian from high school. This experience significantly boosted his confidence, independence, and language skills, and expanded his global perspective, echoing the multicultural exposure he experienced growing up in Buenos Aires. Studying in Paris, often called the "City of Light," mirrored his early fascination with architecture and urban design, connecting his early influences with international perspectives.

Throughout university, he served as a **teaching assistant in technical and architectural drawing and morphology**, the study of shapes, proportions, colors, and textures. He also participated in workshops in Chile and Brazil, collaborating with peers from different countries, and showcased his work in final exhibitions. These experiences honed his skills in both individual excellence and collaborative work, laying a strong foundation for his later transition into **artificial intelligence and deep learning**, where creative design thinking and analytical problem-solving would converge.

## Section 2: Career Journey: From Architecture to AI

The subject's career journey is marked by a transition from architecture to artificial intelligence, showcasing his adaptability and diverse skill set.

After completing his studies, he spent ten months in **New Zealand**, not just traveling but actively moving and working in seasonal jobs. This period honed his adaptability, independence, and ability to navigate unfamiliar environments. Through networking and getting to know people, he secured a position as a **design manager** for a construction company specializing in stone, terracotta, and steel cladding. His responsibilities combined office-based work and site visits, ensuring that projects were executed according to design plans. Notable projects included the **Kiri Te Kanawa Theatre**, **Aotea Centre**, **Auckland Council buildings**, **Auckland Art Gallery**, and various residential projects. Being one of the youngest members of the team, he took on significant responsibilities early, developing expertise in **team management**, technical drawings, and 2D and 3D detailing for building envelope components.

Personal reasons led him to move to **Germany**, where he immediately started learning German, progressing from **A1 to B1**, before returning to Buenos Aires for the first time in three years to spend Christmas with family. During this trip in **2018**, a family lunch with his brother proved pivotal: his brother demonstrated a **reinforcement learning Python script solving a maze**, showing how an agent could learn autonomously through trial, error, and feedback. Witnessing this process sparked his fascination with machine learning and what we now call AI.

Back in Germany, the onset of the **COVID-19 pandemic** created a unique window for self-directed learning. He first explored machine learning and deep learning independently and then enrolled in **Le Wagon**, completing a **600-hour Data Science & AI and Data Engineering program**. This comprehensive curriculum covered data analytics, decision science, machine learning, deep learning, machine learning engineering, generative AI, ethical AI development, database management, data storage, batch pipeline development, and strategies for handling data at scale.

His rapid grasp of these concepts led to a **lead teaching role at Le Wagon**, deepening his understanding of deep learning. His architectural background provided a natural advantage: it helped him intuitively visualize the **multidimensional projections occurring within neural network layers**, similar to parametric design and morphology exercises he had practiced during university. This insight later informed his approach to generative AI and complex systems modeling.

Following his teaching experience, he worked as a **freelance consultant**, specializing in **deep learning pipelines** for computer vision, generative AI, and multi-agent systems with tools and memory, handling image and text applications. He also applied reinforcement learning techniques to **sequential data**, exploring optimization problems across various domains.

Leveraging his architectural background, he applied AI in areas such as:  

- **3D AI for Architecture**: Using **3D Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)** to generate and optimize building designs.  
- **Smart Cities projects**: Applying **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)** reinforcement learning to optimize urban systems, including traffic flow and energy usage.  
- **Prompt-driven robotic and generative systems**: Using **LLMs to remotely control robotic arms or generate structured outputs**, translating natural language prompts into **G-code instructions, 3D designs, or text/image outputs**. This work connects to his early skills in **spatial reasoning, parametric thinking, and visualizing complex structures**, bridging his architectural background with AI applications.  
- **KV-cache streaming in generative workflows**: Implementing systems where logs or real-time events can interrupt an ongoing generation, injecting new context and shifting outputs dynamically. This method can be applied in **e-sports analytics, interactive simulations, or live AI-driven environments**, allowing adaptive responses and context-aware generation.

His career has taken him to major cities worldwide, including **Berlin, Amsterdam, London, Barcelona, Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Kuala Lumpur, Singapore, Tokyo**, and recently **Riyadh**, enhancing his adaptability, communication skills, and global perspective.

Throughout this journey, he has remained committed to **continuous learning and ethical AI development**, following leading researchers, attending conferences, and engaging actively with online AI communities to stay at the forefront of advancements in AI and deep learning.
